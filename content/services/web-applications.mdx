Most custom software problems are not really technical problems. They are about understanding exactly who uses the tool, what they need to see, and which steps can be removed. We spend real time on that before writing code, because it is the cheapest point to get things right.

We build with a deliberately conventional, well-supported stack. That keeps applications fast and reliable, makes them easier to hire for later, and avoids tying your business to unusual tools or to us.

First releases are kept small on purpose. Shipping a focused version quickly, then improving it based on real use, almost always produces a better product than trying to specify everything up front.
