## Overview

Kestrel Roofing is a fictional local roofing company we used to work through a problem many trade and home-service businesses share: a website that exists, but does very little to win work. We designed and built a complete small-business website — homepage, service pages, project gallery, about page and quote request flow — to show how we approach this kind of project.

## The Challenge

Roofing customers usually arrive with an urgent, specific need: a leak, storm damage, or an ageing roof. They are often on a phone, and they want to know three things quickly — does this company do what I need, can I trust them, and how do I get a price?

Typical sites in this space make that harder than it should be. Common problems include slow, heavy pages built on generic themes, vague service descriptions, stock photography that undermines trust, and contact forms buried at the bottom of long pages. The challenge was to design a site that answers those three questions within the first screen on a phone.

## Approach

We started with content structure rather than visuals. Each core service — repair, replacement and inspection — got its own page, written around the questions a homeowner actually asks: what the work involves, roughly how long it takes, what affects the price and what happens after they get in touch.

Trust was handled with things a real business can genuinely provide: licensing and insurance details, written estimates, a gallery of real projects and named team members. We deliberately avoided invented review counts or badges, and designed the testimonial area to remain hidden until real, permission-based reviews exist.

On every page, the next step is visible without scrolling: a "Get a Free Quote" button and a tap-to-call phone number, repeated at natural decision points further down.

## Solution

The finished concept is a lean, statically generated Next.js site with no heavy page builder or plugin layer. Key parts include:

- **A focused homepage** that states what the company does, where it works and how to get a quote in the first screen.
- **Dedicated service pages** with plain-language explanations, a short FAQ and a contextual quote button.
- **A short quote form** asking only for what is needed to call back: name, phone, postcode or ZIP, type of work and an optional photo upload.
- **A project gallery** built from simple content files, so new jobs can be added without touching code.
- **Technical SEO foundations**: unique page titles and descriptions, LocalBusiness and Service structured data, an XML sitemap and clean URLs.

## Technology

The site uses Next.js with static generation, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS and MDX content files, deployed on Vercel. Images are served in modern formats at the right size for each device, and the site ships very little client-side JavaScript.

## Result

Because this is a concept, there are no business metrics to report and we will not invent any. What the project does show is the outcome of the approach: a site where the services, the trust signals and the path to a quote are all clear on a phone screen, with fast-loading pages and a content structure that a small business could keep updated itself. It is a template for how we would approach a real trade or home-service website.
