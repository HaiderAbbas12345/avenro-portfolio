## Overview

This concept explores a common next step for growing service businesses: replacing the spreadsheet, whiteboard and group chat that run daily operations with a purpose-built internal tool. We designed and built a working prototype of a dispatch dashboard for a fictional home-services company with several crews.

## The Challenge

In many service businesses, the daily schedule lives in several places at once — a shared spreadsheet, a calendar, a whiteboard in the office and messages between the office and crews. That works with two crews. With five or six, it starts to break: jobs get double-booked, unassigned work goes unnoticed, and nobody has a reliable picture of the day without phoning around.

The goal was a tool the office manager could keep open all day that answers, at a glance: what is happening today, who is doing it, and what still needs attention.

## Approach

We began by mapping the real workflow rather than the ideal one: how jobs arrive, who assigns them, what changes during the day and what information crews need on site. That produced a deliberately narrow first release focused on one screen — **Today** — instead of a sprawling feature list.

Design decisions followed from that focus. Summary counts sit at the top so problems are visible immediately. A timeline view shows each crew's day across working hours, making gaps and overlaps obvious. Unassigned jobs get their own list with a one-click assign action, because unassigned work is the thing most likely to be forgotten.

## Solution

The prototype is a Next.js application with a PostgreSQL database behind it. It includes:

- **A Today view** with job counts by status, a crew timeline and a queue of unassigned jobs.
- **Job records** with customer details, address, job type, notes and status history.
- **Crew scheduling** with drag-to-reschedule on the timeline and conflict warnings for overlapping jobs.
- **Role-based access**, so office staff can manage the schedule while crews see only their own jobs on mobile.
- **A clean data model** designed to support later additions such as invoicing, customer notifications or reporting.

## Technology

Next.js and React with TypeScript on the front end, server-side data access to a PostgreSQL database, and Tailwind CSS for a consistent component system. The interface is responsive, so crews can use the same application on their phones.

## Result

As a concept, this project has no real-world usage data, and we do not present any. The outcome is a working, well-structured prototype that demonstrates how we approach internal tools: understand the actual workflow first, ship one genuinely useful screen, and design the data model so the application can grow with the business.
